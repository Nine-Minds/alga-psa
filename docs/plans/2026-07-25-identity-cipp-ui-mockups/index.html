Three ways to rebuild the Identity screen

All three carry the same content — pre-consent disclosure, a real connect flow with test-before-save, a contact preflight, single-client piloting, schedule control with pause, visible field rules, a review queue, and run history with client names instead of tenant GUIDs. They differ in where that content lives and what the first run feels like.

Each mockup has a state switcher in the top bar — step through not connected → connected → mapping → preflight → operating → failing. Dark mode toggle on the right. Dialogs, tabs, filters, switches and expanders all work.

1 Guided setup → console recommended

Entra gets its own route. Until the first successful sync, a wizard owns the whole screen — one decision at a time, disclosure before consent, preflight as the gate. After that it becomes a tabbed operations console (Overview · Sync & schedule · Clients · Field rules · Review queue · History · Connection).

Strengths

  • Best first run — the screen has one job at a time
  • Setup chrome disappears forever once you're live
  • Ops console has room for filters, history, per-tenant health
  • Overview leads with "needs your attention"

Costs

  • Two distinct UIs to build and keep consistent
  • New route + mode state; deep links need care
  • Most work of the three

2 One page, ranked by urgency

Stays exactly where it is today — inside the Identity tab, no new route. One scroll, ordered by what matters: a status band that changes character with state, then "needs your attention", then your clients, then everything else collapsed into accordions that state their own summary in the header.

Strengths

  • Cheapest to build; closest to today's structure
  • No navigation, no mode state, no new route
  • Accordion headers carry the answer ("on, every 24 hours")
  • Everything is Ctrl-F findable on one page

Costs

  • First run is still a scroll past things you don't need yet
  • Long page once you have 39 clients and real history
  • Mapping and history stay cramped
Dana: disclosure inlinePeet: preflight present, less prominentSam: pause in the band

3 Client-centric workbench

Flips the object model: the spine is your clients, not the connection. Every row expands in place to show what sync did or would do to that client, with per-client preview and sync. Setup isn't a mode — it's a checklist card that sits above the list until it's done, and unmatched tenants are just rows that need you.

Strengths

  • Piloting one client is the natural default, not a workaround
  • Matches how an owner thinks — by client, not by tenant GUID
  • Failure is diagnosed on the row, with the fix attached
  • Scales cleanly to 200 clients with filters

Costs

  • Weakest cold start — a list is a poor teacher
  • Connection lives in Settings, easy to overlook when it breaks
  • Expanding rows carry a lot; risks becoming dense

Traps removed

  • No "Reconnect" button on a never-connected screen
  • Connection options are real, focusable controls
  • Connection is tested before it is saved
  • Disconnect, create-client and unmap all confirm, and say what survives

  • Preflight before anything writes (the engine already supports it)

  • Run history shows client names, trigger and who ran it
