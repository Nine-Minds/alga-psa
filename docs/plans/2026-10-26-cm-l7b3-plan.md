# L7b3 — Export contributors: time, schedule, meetings, conversations, documents (design charter)

Card 6b6b5ea1. Reference `feature/co-managed-it @ 2d00cb6f0e`. Hard parents: L4b, L6a2, L7b1, L6c, T0-4 (PLAN §5, wave 6),
so the tied-parent note applies: this card always waits for a full wave (DAG-REPORT §3). **No migrations, no entry
points, no schema** — nine new files, all inside seams other cards own. It is small and off the critical path.

## 1. Intent
An export is only worth taking if it contains the work: the hours a customer's staff logged, the schedule they were
booked against, the meetings they attended, the conversations they had and the documents they were given. L7b1 ships the
engine with the core record sets; this card fills in the five operational and engagement domains by registering them as
portable-domain contributors — each one declaring the references it needs, the columns it exports, how it exports, what
it retains and how it validates on the way back. The point of the card is that adding a domain is *registration, not
surgery*: if any of these five needs an engine change, the engine was wrong and L7b1 gets amended before merge.

## 2. What changes, in work order
The order is cheapest-and-most-bounded first, so the contributor contract is exercised by small shapes before the two
awkward ones (meetings, documents).

**Step 1 — operational records: time and schedule.**
`packages/co-managed/src/portableOperationalCatalog.ts` (25) and `portableOperationalExport.ts` (177): `time_entries`,
`time_sheets`, schedule tables (base `TENANT_TABLES_EXPORT_ORDER` in `ee/temporal-workflows/src/activities/
tenant-export-activities.ts` already lists `time_entries`, `time_sheets`, `time_sheet_comments`, `schedules`,
`schedule_entries`, `interactions` — verified in base). Test driver:
`server/src/test/integration/coManagedPortableOperational.cases.ts` (132).
**Step 2 — meetings.** `ee/server/src/lib/co-managed/portableRemoteMeetingExport.ts` (167): Teams meeting metadata,
join URLs and attendance, resolved through the existing EE teams surface (`ee/server/src/lib/teams/` — verified in base)
and the credential vault, not through a new Graph client. Test driver: `coManagedPortableRemoteMeeting.cases.ts` (91).
**Step 3 — conversations.** `packages/co-managed/src/portableConversationExport.ts` (115): ticket and task conversations
with the audience rules L4a/L4b define, tombstones and revisions preserved, private threads filtered by participation.
Test driver: `coManagedPortableEngagement.cases.ts` (117).
**Step 4 — documents and supplemental files.** `portableDocumentCatalog.ts` (20), `portableDocumentExport.ts` (124),
`portableSupplementalFileExport.ts` (90), `portableEngagementCatalog.ts` (18),
`portableEngagementExport.ts` (189). Documents are `documents`, `document_versions`, `document_block_content` (verified
in base migrations); files are blob reads staged through L7b1's `portableBlobStaging`. Test driver:
`coManagedPortableSupplementalFiles.cases.ts` (83).
**Step 5 — registration, not wiring.** Each contributor registers into L7b1's registry at the engine's composition root.
There is no new route, page, action, job, subscriber or workflow.

## 3. Boundaries
- **No engine edits.** L7b1 owns format, manifest, graph, validation, staging, download, the registry itself. If a
  contributor needs a new registry verb, the fix lands in L7b1 before this card merges (stacked cards make that cheap).
- **No restore.** L7b2 owns reading a package back; this card's `validate` hook is the only restore-adjacent surface and
  it must not import restore code.
- **No native admission.** Retained-command admission on appointments, meetings, schedules and interactions is the
  deferred S13 bundle (A-capabilities smell #7; H §2 L7 explicitly excludes it). Do not port `native*.ts` admission
  here or anywhere.
- **No time-domain reimplementation.** Operational time must be read through L6a1's time-source provider registry, not
  by new direct `time_entries WHERE billing_mode='operational'` probes — DNP #16/#17 record exactly that mistake
  (broadened eligibility, unindexed tenant-wide probes, `FOR SHARE` on `tenants`).
- **No new tables**, so no table-manifest row; **no mail**, so no delivery coupling.
- **Exclusions are inherited and must be honoured**: MSP-private content, commercial records and secrets never enter the
  package (H §2 L7 acceptance; PLAN §3 zero-disruption).

## 4. Acceptance
- A: contributor contract per domain — every contributor registers, declares references/columns/export/retain/validate,
  and the manifest names it.
- A: the four integration case files above pass, plus `helpers` reuse from L7b1; a domain absent from a package (e.g.
  tenant with no meetings) yields an explicit exclusion reason, not a silent gap.
- A: conversation export respects audience — a customer-private thread is present for the customer, absent for the MSP
  side of the package, per the L4a/L4b rules; tombstoned content keeps its tombstone.
- A: documents export all versions with digests and respect the size cap; supplemental files never escape their tenant
  path scope.
- A: no entry points added — the L0 gate-coverage contract must show no new match (run it, don't assume); the flag-off
  and schema-absent suites stay green; no migration lint run is needed.
- A: hot-path budget unchanged: nothing here is reachable from a psa request path.
- R: the L7 guide's step 8 (export workspace, inspect manifest) is where this card is visible — five domains named in
  the manifest, with exclusions listed. No separate video.
- Honest gap: the meetings case needs a Teams test app or recorded Graph fixture. If the fixture cannot reach Graph, the
  case runs against a stubbed resolver and that limitation is stated in the charter — it is not a pass.

## 5. Risks
1. **Registry insufficiency discovered late.** L7b3 is where abstract verbs meet real shapes (document versions, meeting
   attendance, thread audiences). Mitigation: L7b1 ships the stub-contributor contract test; L7b3 starts with the two
   smallest domains and raises an engine amendment before Step 3, not after.
2. **Accidental native coupling.** Importing L6c/L6a2/L4b service internals reads as convenience and drags hot-path
   behaviour in. Read through the registries those cards provide.
3. **Audience leakage in conversation export** is the worst outcome of the whole L7 track: exporting an MSP-private note
   into a customer's package. One resolution function, called once, with a test that asserts absence by name.
4. **Billing-visible time.** `time_entries` carries `billing_mode`; exporting commercial rows into a customer package
   leaks commercial terms. Filter to the operational set the domain owns.
5. **Blob volume.** Document versions can dwarf every other domain; cap per-file and per-package, and fail closed with a
   named exclusion rather than a truncated file.

## 6. Open design choices
- **Decided (DAG-REPORT R15, PLAN §5):** the five domains live here; the registry lives in L7b1; parents are L4b, L6a2,
  L6c, L7b1, T0-4.
- **Decided (H §2 L7, A-capabilities #7):** no retained-command admission, no second SLA/export machinery.
- **Open — document scope.** All versions with digests vs latest only. **Leaning** all versions, capped, because a
  package without history is a worse artifact than one with exclusions.
- **Open — meetings content.** Metadata and links only, or transcripts/recordings. **Leaning** metadata and links in v1;
  recordings are large binaries with their own retention rules.
- **Open — conversation granularity.** Participation-evidence only (mirroring the L7 archive rule) vs everything the
  customer can already see. **Leaning** everything the audience can see at cutoff, with post-cutoff state excluded.
- **Open — schedule depth.** Current schedule definitions only, or exceptions and history. **Leaning** definitions plus
  exceptions, no historical revisions.
